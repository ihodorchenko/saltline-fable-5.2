Second owner-provided reference video. This document is the working map: what is
in the video, at which timestamp, what it teaches, and what GLASSLINE can do with
it. No reference imagery or audio is committed — the stills and the audio remain
in the git-ignored kit next to this file (see `README.md`).

## 3. The movement training — beat by beat

This is the section our tutorial should be compared to. Each mechanic gets a
dedicated *designed space*, not a generic roof:

| Beat | Space | Lesson for GLASSLINE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Run / sprint (1:52–2:30) | wide railed white roof, VO prompt | teach the basic run with no obstacles and a clear view of the next landmark; sprint is introduced with a red-and-white "ПРОБЕГ / Shift" prompt |
| Vault / climb (2:30–2:45) | blue box course with a red pad | props at knee/chest height read as vaultable without markers; the red pad marks the landing |
| Jump / ramp (2:45–3:05) | red tubular and curved ramps over the gaps | **red marks the intended action line**; a long, clean run-up makes the jump a reward, not a gamble |
| Balance (3:05) | red pipe on a white roof | the beam is a distinct object, not a painted line; A/D prompt in the subtitle bar |
| Climb / stairs (3:15) | yellow staircase to the upper courtyard | vertical route is readable because the structure *is* the route |
| Interior transition (3:45) | white doorway → red-awning service room → red balcony | a doorway is enough to make a course feel multi-level; the interior is bright, not a dark tunnel |
| Wall-run (4:10) | long white wall with orange/yellow accents | the wall-run surface is visually distinct from the walkable floor |
| Service corridor (4:20) | white alley, stairs, exit | connective tissue that keeps the course from being "roofs only" |

Structure to borrow: **each mechanic in its own purpose-built pocket, separated by
short connective runs**, with color-coded functional props and VO subtitles
instead of floating tutorial text.

### Visual vocabulary confirmed by this video

- Palette: **white concrete/plaster + deep blue glass + saturated red functional
  accents + orange safety elements** (no green, no brown; shadows are blue).
- Props: solar PV arrays, large AC units with circular fans, glass skylights,
  black roll doors, red ladders/stairs/pipes/railings, white lockers, orange
  fencing, billboards, roof-edge parapets.
- Materials: matte white cast concrete, brushed metal frames, dark reflective
  glass with pale mullions, painted steel for the red elements.
- Light: high sun, crisp hard shadows, white surfaces blowing out slightly while
  shaded sides stay blue.

(`docs/visual-gap/metrics-prologue.json`; cutscene/combat frames excluded via

| luma std | 0.259 | 0.272 | 0.253 | close |

| luma p95 | 0.897 | 0.979 | 0.954 | prologue clips less |
| near-white | 24.1 % | 27.3 % | **12.6 %** | **both references have about twice our white area** |
| deep shadow | 10.1 % | 3.0 % | 3.7 % | prologue is much moodier (interiors, shade) |
| saturation | 0.339 | 0.359 | 0.327 | ours slightly under both |
| local contrast | 0.0496 | 0.0713 | 0.064 | ours closer to the tutorial |
| edge energy | 0.0221 | 0.0341 | 0.0273 | ours in between |
| sky-blue area | 44.0 % | 53.1 % | 43.4 % | ours matches the prologue |
| top-third contrast | 0.0432 | 0.0585 | 0.0597 | ours matches the tutorial, above the prologue |
| split-tone dark R−B | −24.2 | −48.6 | −45.0 | prologue is **less blue** in the dark; our look is tutorial-flavoured |
| red accent area | 2.94 % | 4.20 % | 2.07 % | both references use more red than we do |
| colorfulness | 56.2 | 71.1 | 46.3 | both references are more colorful than our build |

**Union of the two references** (a sensible target window for the "Mirror's Edge
look" rather than a single video): luma 0.52–0.59, p25 0.31–0.37, p95 0.90–0.98,
near-white 24–27 %, deep shadow 3–10 %, saturation 0.34–0.36, local contrast
0.050–0.071, edge energy 0.022–0.034, sky blue 44–53 %, top-third 0.043–0.059,
split-tone −49…−24, red accent 2.9–4.2 %.

**Implications:** our tone and composition are inside the reference *range*, but
two content numbers are consistently low in both references — **near-white area
(12.6 % vs 24–27 %)** and **red accent area (2.1 % vs 2.9–4.2 %)**. Both are
level-content decisions (more white roof/deck in frame, more functional red props
in view), not grading knobs.

Note: this video is 720p, 30 fps, ~2.2 Mbps with a watermark, so absolute
acutance/edge numbers are a little lower than the 1080p-class tutorial video; use
it for composition and palette, not for sharpness targets.

## 6. Audio profile

Measured from the extracted 44.1 kHz stereo track (the track contains the game's
mix *plus* Russian voice-over, so treat these as presentation-level numbers):

| Segment | Integrated loudness (approx) | peak dBFS | < 500 Hz | > 2 kHz | Notes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Movement training (1:48–4:40) | −33.4 LUFS | −8.7 | 90.4 % | 2.5 % | quiet narration section; music low |
| Prologue mission (8:20–13:20) | −26.2 LUFS | −2.9 | 93.7 % | 2.6 % | louder, music + wind + footsteps |
| Whole take | −28.4 LUFS | −2.9 | 89.4 % | 5.6 % | — |

Band balance during the mission: 20–120 Hz 67 %, 120–300 Hz 17 %, 300–800 Hz 9 %,
800–2 k 3.6 %, 2–4 k 1.4 %, 4 k+ 1.2 %. The reference is **bass-heavy with a thin
mid/high layer** — the "air" comes from footsteps, cloth and music transients,
not from a constant hiss.

Our current procedural mix (`artifacts/audio/manifest.json`) reaches about
−21.6 LUFS idle / −19.0 sprint with 98.6 % of the ambience below 500 Hz and only
~1–2 % in 800–4000 Hz. So we are in the right family (dark, low-heavy) but even
darker than the reference. **Actionable:** add a very low-level high "air" band
(wind above 2 kHz, gain tied to speed) and make sure the score's arpeggio and
cloth layers sit in 800–4000 Hz — that is what makes the reference mix feel open
without sounding noisy.

## 7. Backlog for GLASSLINE from this video

(Detailed implementation specs, files, effort and acceptance checks for every item:
[`PROLOGUE-BACKLOG.md`](PROLOGUE-BACKLOG.md).)

Prioritised, each item is cheap relative to a full content pass:

**P1 — content vocabulary that is missing**
1. **Solar PV arrays** (instanced: dark-blue panel grid on a frame, 2–3 sizes) on
   the wide roofs. Reference has them everywhere; they add blue/white pattern,
   edge energy and reflecting glints.
2. **Large AC units with circular fan grilles** — we have boxes; the fan detail is
   what makes the reference rooftop read as machinery. Instanced, one draw call.
3. **Glass skylights / roof lights** — a few panels with the new scene
   reflections; they read as glass instantly and break up white decks.
4. **Red functional props**: ladder, handrail, awning, conduit runs. Reference red
   is scattered *in the world*; ours is concentrated in route paint. Adding red
   props closes about a third of the red-accent gap.

**P2 — level structure**
5. **A short service interior** (10–20 s): white corridor + black roll doors +
   lockers + red stair, entered from a roof hatch and exited above — the one
   composition type the reference has that GLASSLINE does not.
6. **Orange safety fencing** as a secondary accent (instanced rails) along roof
   edges and fail boundaries; cheap and instantly reference-flavoured.
7. White-area increase: plant rooms / parapet volumes on the wide roofs so more
   of the frame is white architecture (near-white 12.6 % → target ~18–20 %).

**P3 — polish**
8. Distant life: a helicopter or airship silhouette moving behind the skyline;
   reference uses it to keep the sky alive.
9. Intro/act camera language: the cutscene at 0:31–1:47 is the reference for a
   short opening fly-in over the roofs (we have no cinematic opening yet).
10. Audio "air" band per §6.
